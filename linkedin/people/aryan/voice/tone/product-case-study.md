# Voice: Product Case Study

Post: 2 ("I hate AI that gives me homework") — the only clear example in the source so far.

Read [../dos-and-donts.md](../dos-and-donts.md) first — this file only covers what's specific to this category.

> **First draft, not a settled pattern** — same caveat as Atyab's Behind-the-Scenes voice file. Treat everything below as a reasonable read of one post, to confirm against future examples.

## What this category is for

A narrative case study for one specific paid product (here, Viktor), told as
a real, dated story of using it — distinct from [system-breakdown.md](system-breakdown.md),
which teaches a system rather than reviewing a product, and distinct from the
webinar categories, which sell an event rather than a tool.

## Shape

1. **Open on a skeptical, personal pain-point hook**: *"I hate AI that gives me homework."*
2. **State the skepticism precisely** — what almost-good-enough looks like: *"It can summarize my ad spend, my sales inbox and my billing perfectly. I still have to catch the bad number, write the reply, and decide what gets sent."*
3. **Tell a real, dated, specific story of using the product** — day-by-day, with exact times and numbers, structured like a log: *"Monday, before my flight... Wednesday morning he caught a budget drifting 31%... Thursday he found 3 invoices..."*
4. **One line contrasting this product against "most AI"**: *"Most AI tells me what to do. This one did it, then came back for a yes on the pricing and the invoices."*
5. **Close with a specific, concrete offer and a hard link**, plus — uniquely in this whole corpus — **hashtags**, marking the post as paid/boosted: `#AIemployee #PaidMedia`.

## Register

More narrative and dated than any other category — reads like a log entry, not an argument or a teardown. The hashtags are the single clearest signal in the entire corpus: they appear *only* here, meaning this specific post was paid media, not organic. Don't add hashtags to an organic draft in any other category on this basis alone.

## Checklist

- Is the story dated and specific (real days, real times), not a vague "I've been using it"?
- Does it name a concrete moment the product caught something or held back correctly, not just "it worked well"?
- Is the CTA a specific, concrete offer (a real discount/credit amount), not a vague "check it out"?
- Only add hashtags if this draft is genuinely paid/boosted content — that's what they signal here.
