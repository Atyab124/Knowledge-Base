# Voice: Webinar Pitch / Case-for-Attending

Posts: 1 ("For most engineers, every advancement in AI means less job stability"), 3 ("If you've been paying attention, you know that OpenAI just released GPT-6 Astra"), 4 ("People have been asking why Wednesday's webinar is for technical employees only").

Read [../dos-and-donts.md](../dos-and-donts.md) first — this file only covers what's specific to this category.

## What this category is for

The longest, most argumentative posts in the set — building the full case for
why a specific reader (a technical W2 engineer anxious about AI) needs to
attend a specific upcoming webinar. Unlike [webinar-social-proof.md](webinar-social-proof.md),
these carry the actual argument; the proof-flash posts lean on this one having
already landed.

## Shape

1. **Open on the reader's fear, not his own experience** — the opposite move from a narrative voice: *"For most engineers, every advancement in AI means less job stability."* / *"If you've been paying attention, you know that OpenAI just released GPT-6 Astra..."*
2. **Reframe the threat as a two-tier world**: AI-fluent readers vs. AI-illiterate business owners. The reader is told they're now *more replaceable* to their employer and *more necessary* to a small business — the same fact, told twice, cutting opposite ways: *"You, technically, become more replaceable to your AI-fluent manager, and become more necessary to a small business owner who is AI-illiterate."*
3. **Escalate stakes with direct, repeated "you" address and rhetorical questions**: *"You don't think that's worth $5-8k a month? You don't think they'd rather pay someone like you a small monthly retainer than go belly up by 2028?"*
4. **Handle an objection or qualify the audience explicitly** when relevant (post 4): "this is not for the general public," "this is truly for engineers who want to grow."
5. **Close on urgency addressed straight at the reader**, then the CTA: *"You are needed more than you know."* / *"cannot wait to see you on Wednesday."* — followed by "Sign up / Link is in the comments."

## Register

Longer than any other category here — these are essays, not flashes. Still one thought per line, heavy blank space. Grammar is clean throughout (unlike a looser voice's dropped apostrophes). Numbers appear ($5-8k/month, 90% of business owners, 2028) but sparser than in System Breakdown — this category sells on *argument*, not on *proof*.

## Checklist

- Does it name the reader's specific fear before offering the reframe?
- Is there at least one direct "you" rhetorical question raising the stakes?
- Does the close land on the reader's importance/urgency, not a generic sign-off?
- Save the hard proof numbers for [system-breakdown.md](system-breakdown.md) or [webinar-social-proof.md](webinar-social-proof.md) — this category runs on argument.
