# General Do's and Don'ts — Any Category

For an AI drafting a post in Aryan's voice, regardless of category. Category-specific
tone lives in [tone/](tone/) — read the matching file there first. This document is
only what holds true *across* every category in [categorization.md](categorization.md).

This voice is built for conversion, not reflection — nearly the opposite emphasis
from Atyab's. Don't import Atyab's rules here; verify everything below against
[corpus/posts.md](corpus/posts.md) independently.

Mechanical platform conventions (no em-dashes, no exclamation marks, markdown
doesn't render, one-thought-per-line rhythm) live in
[../../../_shared/platform-conventions.md](../../../_shared/platform-conventions.md)
— load that too, always. This file is only what's specific to Aryan.

## Do

1. **End with an explicit call to action — always.** Every post drives toward one concrete next step: "sign up in the comments," "link in the comments," "DM me," "free access below," "comment X to get access." A post with no ask is the exception (see [tone/technical-opinion.md](tone/technical-opinion.md)), not the default.
2. **Anchor every claim in a specific, real, checkable number.** $100,000+, 15,811 companies, 168 replies, 28% reply rate, 300 engineers, 100 DMs, 30M views, $1M+ business, 51 minutes of run time, $24.30 cost per lead. Never round up, never say "a lot" or "many" when a real figure exists.
3. **Open with a short, punchy declarative line that stands alone as its own paragraph** — a pain point or a contrarian claim: "I hate AI that gives me homework." "I hate building frontends with AI." "Most companies don't lose money because they 'picked the wrong AI tool.'"
4. **Address the reader directly with "you" when making the case.** This is persuasion copy, not narration: "You don't think that's worth $5-8k a month?" "You are needed more than you know."
5. **Structure any system or process as a numbered list, one line of payoff per step** — no paragraphs: "1) Sponsor Radar: ... 2) Buyer Finder: ..."
6. **Use → arrows for compressed proof-point summaries**, placed right after a numbered breakdown: "→ 15,811 brands scored. 600 contacted. 168 replies. 28% reply rate."

## Don't

1. **No hashtags — except the one post explicitly tagged `#PaidMedia`.** Hashtags mark boosted/paid content specifically here, not organic voice. Don't add them to an organic draft.
2. **Don't drop apostrophes or write in sentence fragments.** Unlike some other voices, Aryan's grammar stays fully clean and punctuated even in his most casual-sounding openers ("GM." is casual in *tone*, not in grammar).
3. **Don't end without a CTA.** Only 1 of 11 posts here has zero ask — that's a deliberate, rare exception (pure technical opinion), not something to reach for by default.
4. **Don't claim a number that isn't real and precise.** Every stat is presented as exact: "15,811," not "over 15,000"; "28%," not "nearly a third."
5. **Don't write generic engagement bait ("Thoughts below?", "Agree?").** Every CTA here is specific and transactional — a real next step (register, DM, comment a keyword for access) — never a vague bid for comments.
