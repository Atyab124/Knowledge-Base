# Voice: Sharing Knowledge

Posts: 4 ("What do you think?"), 6 ("LLMs are like women...."), 10 ("Can AI be too smart?"), 11 ("If you walk into an AI interview...").

Read [../dos-and-donts.md](../dos-and-donts.md) first — this file only covers what's specific to this category.

## What this category is for

Teach exactly one generalizable lesson, pulled from something that actually happened, not abstract advice. Every post here names the lesson explicitly by the end: ask "what do you think?" instead of correcting the model; restart sessions before drift compounds; constraint beats intelligence; use AI tools openly, including in interviews.

## Shape

1. **A real, specific situation** — a recurring annoyance (post 4), a failed system (post 10), a live interview (post 11), or a personal habit under strain (post 6). Never a hypothetical.
2. **The turn** — the moment the old approach stopped working or a specific question/challenge reframed it: *"So I stopped correcting it"* (4); *"Then I spoke to someone who builds AI systems for large enterprises. He asked one question:"* (10); *"That's when it clicked."* (10 and 11, verbatim in both).
3. **The lesson, stated plainly**, usually addressed straight at the reader: *"If you live in Claude Code like I do... Ask what do you think more often."* / *"If your AI needs perfect data to work, it's already broken."* / *"if you're still treating Cursor like cheating… you're already behind."*

## Two registers, pick based on how polished the source moment was

**Tight/tactical** (post 4) — short declarative sentences, almost telegraphic, no scene-setting beyond what's needed:
> The model writes something
> Its wrong, or half nonsense, and it doesnt even notice
> Correct it directly and it gets defensive, patches around the mistake, makes it worse

**Narrative/structured** (posts 10, 11) — cleaner grammar, apostrophes intact, dialogue quoted directly, a clear reveal beat, closes on a named portable principle:
> "What happens when multiple tables have the same headers, random names, and completely different purposes?"
> With 2,000+ tables, that's not an edge case
> That's just reality

Post 6 is a third, looser variant: an irreverent, even provocative opener unconnected to the lesson ("LLMs are like women.... / Cant live with, cant live without."), a real technical lesson in the middle (LLMs drift → restart sessions), and a self-deprecating, unresolved close (*"But im lazy. / Very. / So im just baking it into my production framework. / Ill let you know if it works 💀"*). Use this register when the lesson is real but not yet fully proven — admitting that directly is part of the voice, not a hedge to cut.

## Checklist

- Does the post name ONE lesson, not several?
- Is there a real before/after or a real challenge that forced the reframe — not a generic "here's a tip"?
- Does it close by handing the lesson back to the reader as something they can use today?
- If uncertain whether the fix works yet, say so (post 6) rather than overclaiming.
